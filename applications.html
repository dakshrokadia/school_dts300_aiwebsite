Applications of AI

Most people used AI several times today without noticing. Here is where it actually turns up.

  • Recommendations

    Netflix deciding what to show you next, Spotify building a playlist, YouTube picking the next video. These all work the same way: the system looks at what you have watched or listened to, finds other people with similar habits, and suggests what they liked.

    This is probably the most common use of AI in daily life, and it is also the least noticed, because it looks like the app just knowing you.

  • Chatbots and writing tools

    Tools like ChatGPT are built on large language models, trained on huge amounts of text so they can predict what words should come next. That is genuinely all they are doing, which is why they can write a convincing paragraph on a subject they get factually wrong.

    They are used for drafting emails, summarising documents, answering customer service questions, and helping with code. The ethics page covers the problems this creates.

  • Medical imaging

    AI systems are trained on thousands of scans that doctors have already labelled, then used to flag things a human might miss. They are used for spotting tumours, reading eye scans for signs of diabetes, and picking up early signs of disease in X-rays.

    Importantly, these systems assist rather than replace. A doctor still makes the call. The system points at things worth a second look.

  • Self-driving cars

    Cars like Waymo's use cameras and sensors to work out what is around them, then decide what to do about it. The system has to recognise pedestrians, read signs, judge how fast other cars are going, and handle situations nobody specifically trained it for.

    That last part is the hard bit, and it is why fully self-driving cars have taken far longer to arrive than people predicted.

  • Translation

    Google Translate and similar tools got dramatically better around 2016 when they switched to neural networks. Before that they largely translated phrase by phrase. Now they work with the whole sentence, which is why the results stopped sounding quite so much like a robot.

  • Fraud detection

    Banks use AI to spot transactions that do not fit your normal pattern. If your card is suddenly used overseas at 3am, the system flags it. It learned what normal looks like for you, rather than following a fixed rule.

    This is also a good example of the trade-off: make it too sensitive and it blocks real purchases, too relaxed and it misses fraud.

What these have in common

Every example here works by learning from examples rather than following rules somebody wrote, which is the core idea from the what is AI page. None of them understand what they are doing. They are all very good at finding patterns.
